What do you know about mephedrone?
Meph. Meow meow. A party drug?
But did you know that it was sold as plant food?
It was first described in 1929, in a chemistry journal, as a cousin of ephedrine.
Then everybody forgot about it for seventy years.
By 2009, it was sold in British online shops. The label said plant food.
Under it, in small letters: not for human consumption. That one line kept it legal.
No plant ever got any.
That year, in a big survey of clubbers, more than 40% said they had tried it.
The high fades fast, and within an hour or two the brain starts begging for more.
Hearts race. Jaws clench. Sleep disappears for a night or two.
Then comes the crash. Anxiety, emptiness, and a very heavy Monday.
In April 2010, Britain banned it. By the end of that year, so did the whole EU.
In Russia, police seized more than six tonnes of it in 2024 alone.
So a molecule from 1929 got a second life as plant food.
The plants are fine, by the way. They never touched it.
