What do you know about energy drinks?
Sugar, caffeine, and a can that keeps you up until three?
But did you know that the bull in them is real?
In 1827, two German scientists found a new substance in ox bile.
They named it after the Latin word for bull. Taurine.
The internet says it comes from bull semen. The taurine in your can is made in a lab.
The drink came from Thailand. In 1976, a Thai drink with a red bull on the label was sold to truck drivers on night shifts.
An Austrian marketing man tried it to fight jet lag. In 1987, he launched his own version in Austria.
Last year his company sold almost fourteen billion cans.
A small can has about as much caffeine as a cup of coffee. A big one has two.
Plus almost seven teaspoons of sugar.
From April 2027, England will ban selling them to anyone under sixteen.
So a molecule named after bull bile, from a drink for Thai truckers, now sells fourteen billion cans a year.
Somewhere, a bull is still waiting for his royalties.
